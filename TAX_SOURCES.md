# 2026 tax and vesting references

Snapshot **US-W2-2026.1**. Accessed **October 27, 2026**. These are static planning rules for full-year W-2 residents/workers, single or married-joint, standard deduction, under age 50 and no dependents. Subsequent years project this snapshot; future legislation and other tax-return situations are not modeled.

## Federal and benefits

- [IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2025-45_IRB): 2026 standard deductions $16,100 single / $32,200 joint. Marginal bracket upper bounds single: 12,400 / 50,400 / 105,700 / 201,775 / 256,225 / 640,600 / uncapped; joint: 24,800 / 100,800 / 211,400 / 403,550 / 512,450 / 768,700 / uncapped. Rates 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, 37%. Brackets/deductions indexed. The itemized-deduction limitation is not applied to standard deductions.
- [SSA 2026 fact sheet](https://www.ssa.gov/cola/factsheets/2026.html): 6.2% Social Security on up to $184,500 **per person**, not per employer, plus 1.45% Medicare without a wage ceiling. Wage ceiling indexed.
- [IRS Additional Medicare](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc560): 0.9% above fixed combined wage thresholds $200,000 single / $250,000 joint. Employer withholding's $200,000 trigger is not substituted for household liability.
- [IRS 401(k) limits](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500): under-50 elective deferrals $24,500/person, aggregated across employers. Income-tax exclusion does not exclude ordinary deferrals from FICA wages. [Treatment](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc424).
- [IRS HSA limits](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2025-21_IRB): $4,400 self-only / $8,750 family; the family limit is shared. [Household rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969), [qualified cafeteria-plan payroll treatment](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b). Eligibility is user-confirmed for the whole year. No employer contributions/catch-ups are assumed.

## Location-specific income tax

**Ohio / Columbus**

- [Enacted Ohio rates](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5747.02): taxable nonbusiness income ≤$26,050 has zero income tax; above that, $332 + 2.75% of excess. No federal-style standard deduction.
- [Published exemption amounts](https://dam.assets.ohio.gov/image/upload/tax.ohio.gov/forms/ohio_individual/individual/2025/it1040-booklet.pdf), [ORC exemptions](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5747.025): $2,400/person for AGI ≤$40,000; $2,150 ≤$80,000; $1,900 below $500,000; zero at/above $500,000. Published indexed amounts carry into 2026, whose indexing is frozen. [Enacted budget explanation](https://www.lsc.ohio.gov/assets/legislation/136/hb96/en0/files/hb96-tax-greenbook-as-enacted-136th-general-assembly.pdf).
- Basic exemption credit and joint credit are included. [ORC credits](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5747.05).
- [Columbus](https://www.columbus.gov/Government/City-Auditor/Income-Tax-Division/General-Income-Tax-Information): 2.5% qualifying wages, including traditional 401(k) deferrals. Qualified cafeteria-plan health/HSA exclusions are retained. Residence and work are both assumed to be Columbus; cross-city credits and school-district taxes are outside coverage.

**California / San Francisco**

- [FTB October 2026 indexing announcement](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/about-ftb/newsroom/tax-news/): standard deductions $5,900/$11,800 and personal exemption credits $158/$316. Single bracket upper bounds: 11,456 / 27,157 / 42,861 / 59,498 / 75,197 / 384,109 / 460,927 / 768,213 / uncapped. Joint: 22,912 / 54,314 / 85,722 / 118,996 / 150,394 / 768,218 / 921,854 / 1,536,426 / uncapped. Rates 1%, 2%, 4%, 6%, 8%, 9.3%, 10.3%, 11.3%, 12.3%.
- Exemption-credit phaseout starts $260,778/$521,561 are **derived** from published 2025 starts and announced 3.4% indexing, pending the complete 2026 booklet. $6 per personal exemption is removed per $2,500 increment of excess federal AGI. The $2,500 step stays fixed.
- [2026 estimated-tax instructions](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2026/2026-540-es-instructions.html): 1% Behavioral Health Services Tax on taxable income above fixed $1 million, including joint filers.
- [EDD benefits wage-base table](https://edd.ca.gov/siteassets/files/pdf_pub_ctr/de231eb.pdf): HSA deductions are added back for CA income tax and SDI. Qualifying payroll health premiums reduce both; ordinary 401(k) reduces income tax, not SDI. [HSA nonconformity](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-540-ca-instructions.html). HSA interest/dividends would be state-taxable; this model assumes unrealized appreciation, not such distributions.
- [EDD SDI](https://edd.ca.gov/en/Disability/Contribution_Rates_and_Benefit_Amounts): 1.3% with no wage ceiling.

**New York / NYC**

- [Tax Law §601](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/TAX/601): enacted 2026 single/joint schedules and §601(d-5) high-income benefit recapture. These fixed thresholds are not inflated. Explicit 2026 constants are used instead of carrying over 2025 or 2027 recapture values.
- [Standard deduction](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pit/file/standard_deductions.htm): $8,000/$16,050, fixed. [IT-201 instructions](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/current_forms/it/it201i.pdf): NYC resident tax, household credits, and school-tax credits. Printed base amounts are used; continuous schedules can differ slightly from the actual $50 return-table intervals. No refundable amount beyond liability is paid out.
- [WCB 2026 PFL](https://www.wcb.ny.gov/content/main/PressRe/paid-family-leave-2026.jsp): employee contribution 0.432% of gross wages capped at $411.91/person annually. [Optional disability deduction](https://www.wcb.ny.gov/content/main/DisabilityBenefits/employer-disability-benefits.jsp): 0.5% up to $0.60/week ($31.20 annualized), separately toggled. Employer-funded employee shares can be disabled.

**Massachusetts / Boston**

- [DOR rates](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/tax-rates): 5% ordinary income plus 4% surtax above indexed $1,107,750, same threshold for joint filers.
- [Personal exemptions](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/personal-income-tax-exemptions): $4,400/$8,800, fixed. [FICA deduction](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/social-security-fica-and-medicare-deduction): actual Social Security and Medicare up to $2,000/person; unused spouse allowance is not transferred. HSA treatment conforms federally.
- [No Tax Status and Limited Income Credit](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/no-tax-status-and-limited-income-credit): NTS AGI thresholds $8,000/$16,400; LIC ceilings $14,000/$28,700. Within LIC range, modeled income tax is the lesser of regular tax and 10% of AGI above the NTS threshold. Thresholds fixed.
- [PFML employee share](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/paid-family-and-medical-leave-employer-contribution-rates-and-calculator): 0.46% capped at the Social Security wage limit per person annually. [Wage reporting](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/wage-contributions-reporting-for-paid-family-and-medical-leave): employee cafeteria-plan salary reductions remain in the PFML contribution base; employer benefit payments are different.

**Washington / Seattle**

- No wage income tax. [PFML estimator](https://paidleave.wa.gov/estimate-your-paid-leave-payments/): 1.13% × 71.43% employee share = 0.807159%, capped at the Social Security wage limit per person.
- [WAC 192-510-025](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=192-510-025): transferred stock is wages. [Reporting toolkit](https://paidleave.wa.gov/app/uploads/2021/12/Employer-Wage-Reporting-and-Premiums-Toolkit-v24.1-2025.12.12.pdf): PFML and Cares use gross wages. Employee salary reductions are included; employer benefit contributions are not modeled.
- [WA Cares](https://wacaresfund.wa.gov/how-it-works): 0.58%, uncapped. Approved exemptions can be selected separately.

**Texas / Austin:** no employee wage income tax or statewide employee leave/disability premium is added. Federal taxes still apply.

## Projection and coverage caveats

Indexed federal brackets/deductions, Social Security and associated annual caps, contribution limits, CA indexed amounts and the MA surtax threshold grow at the user-selected tax inflation assumption. Fixed thresholds remain fixed. Rate percentages do not grow. This is a projection of 2026 law and deliberately does not claim to predict future tax legislation.

Itemization, dependent/family/earned-income credits, age-based additions, self-employment, foreign income, part-year/cross-state wages, investment distributions/realized gains, tax-return rounding and refundable-credit payouts are not modeled. Explicit final-liability adjustments and the manual effective-rate alternative are available. Totals do not include employer-side payroll taxes.

## Vesting templates

Sources: [general schedule discussion](https://www.levels.fyi/blog/unique-vesting-schedules.html), [Amazon](https://www.levels.fyi/companies/amazon/salaries/software-engineer), [Google](https://www.levels.fyi/companies/google/salaries/software-engineer), [Meta](https://www.levels.fyi/companies/meta/salaries/software-engineer), [Apple](https://www.levels.fyi/companies/apple/salaries/software-engineer), [Microsoft](https://www.levels.fyi/companies/microsoft/salaries/software-engineer). Accessed October 27, 2026. These reported examples have alternatives; none is asserted to apply to every current offer.

Historical Amazon 5/15/40/40 uses month offsets 12:5%, 24:15%, and 30/36/42/48:20% each. A reported annual 5/15/45/35 variant is also offered. Google 38/32/20/10 is distributed monthly or quarterly in selectable templates; a historical 33/33/22/12 monthly variant is included. Meta-style quarterly, Apple-style semiannual and Microsoft-style annual examples distribute 25% per year over four years. Custom rows and cliff templates are editable.

The model uses month-resolution, end-of-month events: offset 1 is the end of the grant month and offset 12 is the end of its 12th month. This convention is explicitly displayed and is not a day-level reconstruction of legal vesting dates. Sign-on cash, options, later share sales, capital gains realization and liquidation taxes are excluded.
