# Offer Evaluation Questions & Variables to Model

This document compiles all the questions you asked while evaluating your Big Tech (Amazon) and startup (General Matter) offers, alongside other company comparisons. These questions highlight the exact variables, blind spots, and trade-offs needed to build a comprehensive career financial calculator.

---

## 1. Startup Equity Mechanics & Valuation Math
Questions focused on cutting through recruiter "Total Compensation" pitches to find real ownership and exit value:
* **Initial Offer Expectations:** Based on company size (e.g., 25 software engineers, ~250 total employees) and stage, what should I expect in terms of the offer?
* **Fully Diluted Percentage:** What exact percentage of the fully diluted share pool does this option grant represent?
* **Company Valuation:** How much is the company worth right now? (Distinguishing between preferred post-money valuation, 409A common stock valuation, and secondary market pricing).
* **Series A Math:** If they raised $50 million in a Series A, wouldn't their valuation be much higher than $50M?
* **409A Strike Price & Discounts:** What is the current 409A strike price per share, and what does a ~75% discount per share mean for my out-of-pocket exercise cost?
* **Current Paper Value:** How much money is a 0.12% grant (or 0.03%, 0.05%, 0.08%, 0.15%) worth right now at the current valuation?
* **Vesting Schedules:** This grant is for 4 years (or stretched to 5 years), right? What are the exact percentages and dollar values vesting per year?
* **Annual Equity Thresholds:** Even 0.15% isn't close to matching Big Tech day-one pay—wouldn't $50k+ of equity a year be a lot more, and what percentage of the company is required to hit that?
* **Typical Startup Benchmarks:** What is typical equity for this role and stage? How much do other Series A startups give for equity?
* **What Offer Would Make It Worth It:** Based on their current valuation and base salary (e.g., $130k with no cash bonus), what exact dollar amount and percentage of equity do I need to ask for to beat Amazon, assuming an exit takes 7+ years?
* **Exit Modeling & Comparisons:** Can you do a table of percent equity and my potential gains after exiting at reasonable price levels, and compare that to staying at Amazon during that time?

## 2. Liquidity, Taxes & Exit Mechanics
Questions about turning private paper stock into spendable cash and avoiding financial traps:
* **Tender Offers:** What is a tender offer, how does it work, and when do startups actually offer them?
* **Post-Termination Exercise Windows:** What are exercise windows, and what happens to my shares if I leave the company before an IPO or acquisition?
* **Loans & Collateral:** Would anyone ever give you personal loans or financing against unexercised startup options or private equity?
* **Early Exercise & Taxes:** Does the option plan allow for early exercise so I can file an 83(b) election and avoid Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT)?

---

## 3. Cash Compensation, Raises & Recruiter Framing
Questions about guaranteed take-home pay, hidden costs, and unwritten promises:
* **Decoding "Competitive":** When a recruiter asks what my competing offer is (~$180k) and says they can be "competitive," what does "competitive" actually mean here?
* **Quarterly & Performance Raises:** They said you get raises and bonuses based on quarterly performance (in both cash and stock), but didn't share numbers and said performance targets probably won't be in the offer letter—how does that actually work?
* **What's Actually in the Offer Letter:** Will all of these equity, valuation, and raise details just be in the written offer letter, or are they left out?
* **Timing the Money Conversation:** Do I ask about compensation numbers during the interview loop, or wait until I get a formal offer?
* **Post-Rotational / Future Pay:** For rotational programs (like GMO), what is the full-time pay after the 2-year period ends, and is it worth doing over Amazon?

---

## 4. Big Tech Benchmarks, Growth & Opportunity Cost
Questions evaluating the baseline guaranteed offer, stock growth, and whether to keep interviewing or renege:
* **Baseline Offer Comparison:** I received my return offer from Amazon ($187k Year 1, $177k Year 2). Is it even worth continuing to interview with companies like P&G, JPMorgan Chase, Capital One, and General Matter?
* **Big Tech Stock Growth:** Assuming Amazon grows at ~10% to 20% a year, how does its stock growth rate affect my 4-year expected value, and how much does a startup need to offer for the expected value to be the same?
* **Tier-1 Tech Comparisons:** What is Meta New Grad (E3) and Google New Grad (L3) looking like pay-wise compared to Amazon, and how does job security compare (especially since Apple has a reputation for being rough)?
* **Reneging Criteria:** There's no way I'd renege on Amazon for a Series A startup, right—it's just not worth it? Is there *any* company (e.g., quant trading, frontier AI labs, Tier-1 tech) that would be worth reneging for?
* **Evaluating Pay Splits:** When given multiple offer splits (e.g., Max Equity vs. Balanced vs. Max Cash with varying strike prices and 5-year vests), does the financial upside justify the risk and extra hours?

## 5. Career Trajectory, Growth & Risk Tolerance
Questions weighing long-term wealth goals (like hitting $1M by 30) against startup learning and career speed:
* **Headcount & Company Growth:** If a startup plans to 2x or 3x in employees over the next couple of years, what will growth look like compared to Amazon?
* **Startup Promotions:** How do startup promotions typically work compared to Big Tech level ladders?
* **Career Trajectory & Self-Assessment:** How do startups compare career trajectory-wise? What questions should I ask myself to figure out what I want (e.g., balancing entrepreneurship interests and learning breadth against hitting $1M net worth by age 30)?
* **Timing Risk:** Is right out of college the right time to take a startup risk, or is it better to bank Big Tech cash first?
* **Part-Time Startup Work:** Is working 20 hours a week at a low-paying ($20/hr) local startup during school worth the burnout if I'm already working 50–60 hours on classes and recruiting?

---

## 6. Questions to Ask Founders, Recruiters & VCs
Questions you sought out to ask company insiders, hiring managers, and local venture capitalists to uncover red flags:
* **Questions for the Startup Recruiter/Team:** What specific questions do I need to ask them about the offer, equity, and company before making a decision?
* **Questions for On-Site Interviews:** Since I don't know who will interview me, what are genuinely interesting questions to ask engineers, leads, and founders—and is it appropriate to ask about the company's business itself?
* **The "5 Key Things":** What are the 5 key things I need to learn about the startup before I leave my on-site visit so I can make an informed decision?
* **Perks & Benefits:** What perks do they offer—do they provide things like free food or a gym stipend?
* **Questions for Founders & VCs:** When networking with startup founders and local venture capital EIRs/analysts (e.g., at the Keenan Center), what should I ask them about how early-stage equity and dilution actually work?
* **Questions for Enterprise/Big Tech Interviewers:** What are 5 good questions to ask about the company and the interviewer (e.g., at P&G) that recruiters actually like?
